Press play and watch the track through six meters modelled on the metering plugins producers use while mixing. Click the waveform to jump around, switch the spectrum to mid/side to see what sits in the stereo field, or press Mic to visualize your own room.

What the meters show
  • Waveform: the whole track. Colour shows where the energy sits: indigo for bass, orange for mids, cream for highs.
  • Spectrum: loudness at each frequency right now, tilted 4.5 dB per octave so a balanced mix looks roughly flat. M/S splits it into what both speakers share (mid) and what differs between them (side).
  • Stereometer: each dot plots the left sample against the right. A vertical line is mono; a wide cloud is wide stereo. The bar below is the correlation, near +1 for mono-safe audio and negative when channels cancel.
  • Peak / LUFS: sample peaks per channel, plus loudness measured the way streaming services do (ITU-R BS.1770). Momentary averages 0.4 s, short-term 3 s, integrated the whole listen.
  • Spectrogram: the spectrum scrolling through time, so drops, risers and filter sweeps leave visible shapes.
  • Oscilloscope: the raw waveform over about 50 milliseconds.
